# ManaGEM — Cinematic Campaign: Concepts and Production Treatment

Client: GEMIS · Product: ManaGEM · Format: 30-second commercial film with 15-second cutdown
Status: concept stage. No video prompts or generation yet.

> **Research caveat.** managem.co.za could not be reached from the working environment
> (the host is outside the session's allowed network domains) and web search returned
> nothing on the product. Everything below is built on the brief: ManaGEM brings
> enquiries, quotations, projects, stock and finances into one place. Every product
> moment must be checked against the real interface before production, and no feature,
> price or statistic has been invented.

---

## 1. Campaign foundation

**The insight.** In most owner-run South African businesses the system is a person.
The quote is in someone's email, the stock count is in the storeman's head, the job
status is on a whiteboard, and the only one who can join the dots is the owner. The
owner is not running the business. The owner is the filing cabinet.

**The proposition.** *Your business shouldn't run you. You should run your business.*
We keep it. It is true, it is spoken the way owners speak, and none of the large
accounting or ERP brands in the market own it.

**Sign-off for all executions.** ManaGEM. Run your business.

**Tone.** Observed, never mocking. The owner is competent and respected; the chaos is
structural, not personal. Humour comes from recognition. No actor ever looks at the
camera and no one ever explains software.

**Craft rules that keep this a film rather than "content".**

1. One location world per film, dressed with real wear: scuffed floors, a faded
   safety poster, a kettle, a wall calendar from a supplier.
2. Motivated light only. Windows, roller doors, fluorescents, a laptop screen.
3. Handheld or Steadicam at eye level. No drone flyovers, no orbiting hero shots.
4. The product appears as a screen in someone's hand or on a desk, shot in-camera,
   glanced at for under two seconds. No floating UI, no icons, no feature list.
5. Dialogue is short, overlapping, and partly off-camera. People interrupt.
6. One supered line at the end. Nothing on screen before it.

## 2. Concept A — "Please Hold"

**1. Campaign title.** Please Hold

**2. Central creative idea.** The commercial happens in real time while a client waits
on hold. A customer ready to sign asks three simple questions: the price, the stock,
the start date. The owner needs "one second". We spend that second with him, to tinny
hold music, as he hunts through a spreadsheet, WhatsApp, the storeroom and a
whiteboard. The call drops. Then the same call again, with ManaGEM: three answers in
one breath.

**3. Target audience.** Owners and operations heads of 5–80 person businesses that
quote, hold stock and deliver jobs: installers, contractors, fabricators, suppliers,
service firms. Age 32–55. They sell on the phone and lose sleep over follow-ups.

**4. Emotional hook.** The specific dread of not knowing your own business in front of
a customer. Every owner has said "let me come back to you" and felt the deal cool.

**5. Storyline.** Sizwe runs a commercial solar and electrical installation company in
Midrand. A property group's procurement manager phones: the board has approved, she
just needs price, stock and a start date. He puts her on hold. The price is in a
spreadsheet with three "final" versions. The stock is forty panels nobody has counted.
The start date is half-erased on a whiteboard. The hold music stops. She has gone.
We reset: same phone call, same yard. This time he glances at a tablet and answers
before she has finished asking.

**6. Opening five seconds.** Black. A phone rings twice. Cut to an extreme close-up of
a cracked-cover phone on a bakkie bonnet, on speaker.
CLIENT (phone): "Sizwe, we're ready to sign. Just confirm the price, the stock, and
when you start."
SIZWE: "Sure. One second."
His thumb presses HOLD. Thin electronic hold music begins.

**15-second cutdown.** 0:00–0:03 the question and HOLD. 0:03–0:08 compressed search
(laptop, storeroom, whiteboard) and CALL ENDED. 0:08–0:12 the reset and the one-breath
answer. 0:12–0:15 end card and VO: "ManaGEM. Run your business."

**8. Scene-by-scene breakdown.**

- *Scene 1, yard, late afternoon.* The question. Establishes the stakes in one line.
- *Scene 2, office to storeroom to whiteboard, continuous.* The search. One unbroken
  take is what makes the wait feel real; the audience is on hold with the client.
- *Scene 3, the phone.* CALL ENDED. The only static frame in the first half.
- *Scene 4, yard, morning.* The reset. Identical framing to Scene 1 so the change
  reads instantly.
- *Scene 5, end card.* Black, type, logo.

**9. Cinematography and camera direction.** First half: a single 12-second Steadicam
take on a 35mm lens, close behind and beside Sizwe, slightly too close for comfort,
whip-panning to each person he asks. Shutter at 90 degrees for a faint nervous edge.
Second half: locked-off tripod, 50mm, clean composition, one slow push-in on his face
as he answers. The camera calms down when he does. Anamorphic 2.39:1 master with a
protected 9:16 and 1:1 for social.

**10. Lighting and production design.** Half one is 16:30 highveld light: hard, low,
dusty, mixed with green-tinged office fluorescents. Half two is 07:30: soft, cool,
clean. Same location, different day, and the grade lifts half a stop. Design details:
a quote printout with a coffee ring, a lever-arch file labelled "QUOTES 2025 (NEW)",
branded workwear that has been washed many times, a stack of panel boxes with
handwritten counts in marker.

**11. Characters and casting.**

- *Sizwe Mokoena, 44, owner.* Built the company from one bakkie. Capable, proud,
  tired. Cast a theatre-trained actor with a lived-in face who can play panic without
  moving much. Not a model.
- *Lerato, 29, office administrator.* Sharp, loyal, drowning. Heard more than seen.
- *Storeman, 50s.* Unhurried. His counting is the comic metronome.
- *The client (voice only), 40s.* Brisk, courteous, senior. A real corporate
  procurement voice, not an advertising voice.

**12. Voiceover and dialogue.** Dialogue as scripted above. VO is a single female
voice, South African, mid-40s, dry and unhurried: someone who has run a business.
Two VO lines only.

**13. Music and sound design.** The score is the hold music. Commission an original
eight-bar tune, first heard as a thin phone-speaker loop, then silence, then the same
melody played by a real band (guitar, bass, drums, a touch of horns, South African
jazz warmth) over the end card. The tune becomes the brand's audio signature and the
radio ad writes itself. Sound design carries the tension: footsteps, a filing drawer,
box counting, then total silence at CALL ENDED.

**14. Product integration.** One screen, twice glimpsed, in the reset. The tablet
shows a single job record in which the quote total, the stock reservation and the
scheduled start are visible together. That one frame is the entire product
demonstration: three answers, one place. The 30-second online version can hold the
screen a beat longer; the landing page continues from exactly that screen.
*To confirm with GEMIS: that a quote, its stock position and its project date can
genuinely be seen on one screen or within one tap.*

**15. Closing message and CTA.** Super: *Your business shouldn't run you.*
Logo and line: *ManaGEM. Run your business.* CTA: *Book a demo · managem.co.za*

## 3. Concept B — "Ask Thandi"

**1. Campaign title.** Ask Thandi

**2. Central creative idea.** In this business every question has the same answer:
"Ask Thandi." We hear it in reception, on the factory floor, in the storeroom, in
accounts. Then we meet Thandi, who is sitting in a school hall with a phone that will
not stop vibrating. When the owner is the system, the business runs her. The
resolution is the first day nobody asks her anything.

**3. Target audience.** Founder-owners of established small and mid-sized firms,
especially those with 10–50 staff, where growth has outrun the owner's ability to
hold everything. Skews 38–55. Strong resonance with women owners and with
second-in-commands who recognise the bottleneck.

**4. Emotional hook.** Being indispensable feels like success until it becomes a trap.
The film names a thing owners are quietly proud of and quietly exhausted by.

**5. Storyline.** A signage and print company in Durban. A customer asks reception
where her order is. A machine operator asks which job is next. A storeman wonders if
vinyl was ordered. A bookkeeper wants to know if a client has paid. Each gets the same
two words. Thandi, the owner, is at her daughter's prize-giving, phone lit with
missed calls, and she steps out as her daughter's name is called. Later: the same
customer, the same question, and the receptionist simply answers. Thandi walks
through her own factory with a coffee and nobody stops her.

**6. Opening five seconds.** A reception counter. A customer in a hurry.
CUSTOMER: "Hi, where's my order?"
RECEPTIONIST (already reaching for the phone): "Let me ask Thandi."
Hard cut to the factory floor mid-sentence: "—ask Thandi."

**7. Complete script (30 seconds).**

| --- | --- | --- |
| 0:00–0:03 | Reception. Customer leans on the counter. Receptionist lifts the handset. | CUSTOMER: "Where's my order?" RECEPTIONIST: "Let me ask Thandi." |
| 0:03–0:10 | Four fast scenes, cut on the words. Print floor: operator holding two job cards. Storeroom: storeman beside an empty rack. Accounts: bookkeeper with a statement. Loading bay: driver with no delivery note. | OPERATOR: "Which one's first?" COLLEAGUE: "Ask Thandi." STOREMAN (to himself): "Ask Thandi." BOOKKEEPER: "Has he paid?" / "Ask Thandi." **15-second cutdown.** Three "Ask Thandi"s (0:00–0:05), the school hall and VO line
(0:05–0:09), "Check ManaGEM" (0:09–0:12), end card (0:12–0:15).

**8. Scene-by-scene breakdown.**

- *Scenes 1–5, the chorus.* Reception, print floor, storeroom, accounts, loading bay.
  Each maps to one thing the product holds: enquiries, projects, stock, finances,
  deliveries. Nobody says those words.
- *Scene 6, school hall.* The cost. This is the heart of the film.
- *Scene 7, reception again.* The answer arrives without her.
- *Scene 8, print floor.* The line that names the product.
- *Scene 9, school hall again.* The reward. Four seconds, no dialogue.

**9. Cinematography and camera direction.** The chorus is shot handheld on a 40mm,
with cuts landing on the "T" of Thandi so the edit has a percussive rhythm. The school
hall is the opposite: a long lens (135mm) from across the hall, shallow, still, her
face among fifty others. The second reception scene repeats the first frame exactly,
then racks focus to Thandi in the background, the only rack focus in the film.

**10. Lighting and production design.** The factory is practical-lit: skylights, the
blue-white of UV print beds, a warm lamp in accounts. The school hall is tungsten
stage light and dusk through high windows, the warmest palette in the film, so that
the place she is missing is also the most beautiful place we see. Design: a wall of
job cards on bulldog clips, vinyl offcuts, a laminated "ASK THANDI BEFORE ORDERING"
sign taped to a stock cupboard (visible in the storeroom scene, gone in the final act).

**11. Characters and casting.**

- *Thandi Khumalo, 46, owner.* Warm authority. People ask her because she always
  knows. Cast for stillness and intelligence; she has three seconds of screen time to
  make us feel nine years of this.
- *Receptionist, 24.* Bright, slightly anxious, first job.
- *Operator, 35.* Deadpan. Delivers "Check ManaGEM" as if it has always been obvious.
- *Intern, 20.* Earnest.
- *Zinhle, 11.* Thandi's daughter. Real, unpolished, searching the crowd.

Cast across Durban's actual mix: isiZulu, English and Indian South African speakers,
with natural code-switching in the background lines.

**12. Voiceover and dialogue.** As scripted. Background lines may drift into isiZulu
("Buza uThandi") for one of the chorus beats; it will read without subtitles. VO: the
same dry female voice as Concept A, two lines.

**13. Music and sound design.** No music for the first ten seconds; the repeated
phrase is the rhythm, with machine noise underneath. A single sustained piano and
cello figure enters under the school hall. It resolves into a light, warm groove from
0:17. The phone vibration is designed as a recurring low motif and is absent from the
last scene.

**14. Product integration.** Two glances. First, the receptionist's monitor: an order
record showing status and delivery date, seen over her shoulder for a second. Second,
the operator's wall-mounted screen or tablet with the day's job queue. The product is
never demonstrated; it is simply what people look at instead of phoning Thandi.

**15. Closing message and CTA.** Super: *Your business shouldn't run you.*
Logo and line: *ManaGEM. Run your business.* CTA: *Book a demo · managem.co.za*

## 4. Concept C — "The Book"

**1. Campaign title.** The Book

**2. Central creative idea.** For thirty-one years a family business has lived in one
battered counter book and one man's memory. His daughter watches him hesitate over a
customer's name for the first time. The film is about what a business is worth when
it exists in one person's head, and about moving it somewhere it can last.

**3. Target audience.** Family-owned and founder-led businesses facing succession or
scale: wholesalers, engineering suppliers, hardware and building merchants. Two
viewers at once: founders aged 55 and over, and the 30–45 year-old sons, daughters
and managers who will have to carry it.

**4. Emotional hook.** Legacy. Pride in what was built, and the unspoken fear that it
could disappear with the person who built it.

**5. Storyline.** An engineering supplies business in Pietermaritzburg. Mr Naidoo
knows every customer, every part number, who pays on the 25th. It is all in the book,
and in him. One morning a regular asks about an order and he opens the book, turns a
page, turns it back, and cannot find it. His daughter Priya sees. That night she sits
alone in the shop entering the book into ManaGEM, page by page. In the morning she
hands him a tablet. He scrolls through thirty-one years, says nothing, then snaps the
rubber band back onto the book and puts it in the drawer.

**6. Opening five seconds.** Extreme close-up: old hands pull a thick rubber band off
a swollen hardcover counter book. Pages fat with staples, invoices, pencil.
PRIYA (VO): "My father ran this business for thirty-one years from one book."

**7. Complete script (30 seconds).**

| Time | Picture | Sound |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 0:00–0:05 | ECU hands and rubber band. The book opens. Columns of handwriting. | Rubber band snap. Paper. PRIYA (VO): "My father ran this business for thirty-one years from one book." |
| 0:05–0:10 | Shop counter, morning. Mr Naidoo serves three customers at once without looking anything up. Priya watches from the stockroom door. | MR NAIDOO: "Forty bearings for Mrs Dlamini. She pays on the twenty-fifth. Always." PRIYA (VO): "And one memory." |
| 0:10–0:14 | A regular at the counter. Mr Naidoo opens the book, turns a page, turns back. His finger stops moving. A long two seconds. Priya sees it. | CUSTOMER: "The order from last week, Uncle?" Silence. A fan turns. MR NAIDOO (quietly): "Last week…" |
| 0:14–0:19 | Night. Shop shuttered. Priya alone at the counter, the book open beside a laptop, typing. A mug gone cold. She turns a page. | Keyboard. Street sounds through the shutter. Piano enters. |
| 0:19–0:25 | Morning. She puts a tablet on the counter in front of him. He scrolls: customers, orders, stock. He stops on one name and looks up. | MR NAIDOO: "It's all here?" PRIYA: "It's all here, Pa." |
| 0:25–0:27 | He puts the rubber band back on the book, opens the drawer, lays the book inside, closes it. | VO: "What you've built should outlast what you can remember." |
| 0:27–0:30 | Wide: father and daughter behind the counter as the shutter rolls up on a new day. Cut to end card. | VO: "ManaGEM. Run your business." |

**15-second cutdown.** The book opens with VO (0:00–0:04), the hesitation
(0:04–0:08), "It's all here, Pa" (0:08–0:11), VO line and end card (0:11–0:15). The
cutdown works but loses much of the feeling; this concept wants 45 or 60 seconds.

**8. Scene-by-scene breakdown.**

- *Scene 1, the book.* An object as protagonist.
- *Scene 2, the counter, morning.* His mastery. We must admire him before we worry.
- *Scene 3, the hesitation.* The whole film turns on two seconds of an actor's face.
- *Scene 4, night.* Her labour of love. The only product-building scene.
- *Scene 5, the tablet.* Father and daughter. Five words.
- *Scene 6, the drawer.* The book is retired, not thrown away.
- *Scene 7, shutter up.* Continuity.

**9. Cinematography and camera direction.** Classical and patient. Tripod and slow
dolly, 50mm and 85mm, nothing handheld. Macro lens for the book. The hesitation is
held in an unbroken medium close-up; we do not cut away to spare him. The night scene
is one slow lateral dolly along the counter.

**10. Lighting and production design.** Morning sun through a dusty shopfront with
hand-painted signage, shafts across steel shelving. Night is one desk lamp and the
laptop's glow. Palette of oiled steel, brown paper, faded green enamel. The shop has
not been redecorated since 1994 and is immaculate. A framed photograph of the opening
day. A calculator with a paper roll.

**11. Characters and casting.**

- *Mr Naidoo, 68.* Founder. Dignified, exact, gently funny. Cast a veteran South
  African stage actor.
- *Priya Naidoo, 34.* Came back from a corporate job to join him. Observant,
  protective, decisive.
- *Customers.* Three regulars who have clearly been coming for twenty years.

**12. Voiceover and dialogue.** Priya narrates the opening in her own voice. The
closing VO is the campaign voice. Dialogue as scripted, minimal.

**13. Music and sound design.** Solo piano, sparse, entering only at the night scene,
joined by strings on "It's all here, Pa". Before that, only the shop: fan, till,
bearings poured into a paper bag, the rubber band. The rubber-band snap bookends the
film.

**14. Product integration.** Two moments. The laptop at night, where handwriting on
the page and the same entry on screen share a frame. Then the tablet in his hands: a
customer record with order history, readable for one beat. The product is positioned
as the keeper of what the business knows.

**15. Closing message and CTA.** Super: *What you've built should outlast what you can
remember.* Logo and line: *ManaGEM. Run your business.* CTA: *Book a demo ·
managem.co.za*

## 5. Evaluation

Scores out of 10, on the brief's five criteria. These are creative judgements, not
research results; they should be pressure-tested with a few real business owners.

| Criterion | A · Please Hold | B · Ask Thandi | C · The Book |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Originality | 9 | 8 | 7 |
| Emotional impact | 8 | 8 | 10 |
| Brand positioning | 8 | 10 | 8 |
| Product clarity | 10 | 8 | 6 |
| Potential to generate enquiries | 9 | 8 | 6 |
| **Total** | **44** | **42** | **37** |

**A · Please Hold.** The real-time hold is a formal device no competitor has used,
and it makes the viewer feel the wait instead of being told about it. The three
questions cover quotes, stock and projects without a feature list, and the
before-and-after is unmissable even with the sound off. It ties the product directly
to lost revenue, which is what moves a decision-maker to book a demo. Weakness: it
dramatises one moment of pain rather than the whole proposition, so the end line has
to do the work of widening it.

**B · Ask Thandi.** The purest expression of "your business shouldn't run you" and
the easiest to extend: every business has its own Thandi, and the phrase can travel
into social, radio and outdoor. Weakness: it needs nine scenes and a large cast in
30 seconds, so it is the hardest to execute without feeling rushed, and the product
benefit is implied rather than shown.

**C · The Book.** The most moving film and the one most likely to be remembered and
shared. It speaks to a large, under-addressed audience of family businesses.
Weakness: it sells the idea of going digital more than it sells ManaGEM, it needs
more than 30 seconds, and its call to action is the softest of the three.

## 6. Recommendation

**Lead with Concept A, "Please Hold".**

1. **It is built for the objective.** The campaign has to generate qualified
   enquiries. This is the only concept where the problem shown is money walking out
   of the door, and the only one where the viewer sees exactly what the product does
   in a single frame.
2. **The idea is the structure.** The hold is not a joke laid over an explainer; the
   real-time wait is the film. That is what separates a commercial from a product
   video.
3. **It is the most producible at a premium level.** One location, three speaking
   roles, one hero long take, and a client who is only a voice. The key line of the
   resolution is delivered while the actor looks away from the screen, and most other
   dialogue is off-camera, which keeps performance and sync risk low whichever
   production route is chosen.
4. **It scales down cleanly.** The 15-second version keeps the entire idea. The hold
   tune gives radio, audio streaming and even ManaGEM's own switchboard a branded
   asset.
5. **It opens a campaign, not a one-off.** Each sequel is a different call and a
   different sector: a caterer asked whether she can do 300 covers on Saturday, a
   fabricator asked whether the steel has arrived.

**How the other two are used.** "Ask Thandi" is the natural second flight and social
series once "Please Hold" has established the product. "The Book" is held as a longer
brand film for a later moment, such as a family-business or succession-themed
partnership.

## 7. What is needed before production

1. **Access to the product.** A demo login or screen recordings of the quote, stock
   and project views, so the hero screen in the reset scene is real and accurate.
2. **Confirmation of the claim.** That price, stock availability and scheduled start
   can be seen together, or within one tap, for a single job.
3. **Brand assets.** Logo, colours, typeface and any existing tagline or tone
   guidelines for ManaGEM and GEMIS.
4. **The demo journey.** The exact landing page and booking flow behind
   "Book a demo", so the film hands over to a page that continues the same story.
5. **Media plan.** Where the film will run (television, cinema, YouTube, LinkedIn,
   Meta), which fixes durations and aspect ratios.
6. **Approval of the concept.** Once one concept is chosen, the next deliverables are
   the shooting script, shot list, storyboard frames and only then the video prompts.
