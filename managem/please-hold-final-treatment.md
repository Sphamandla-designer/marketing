## 0. What changed since Round 1, and why

Round 1 was written without sight of the product. This round is written against the
ManaGEM website source, its October 2026 content audit and the product screens in the
`sphamandla-designer/managem` repository. (The published github.io page itself is
blocked by this environment's network policy; the repository behind it was read
instead, and it is the same content.)

Four things in the Round 1 film were wrong and have been removed.

| Round 1 | What the product material shows | Consequence |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Owner reads the answer off a tablet on a bakkie bonnet | The product is desktop-width only; the site deliberately shows no phone or tablet frames | The product now appears only on an office monitor |
| "The stock's reserved" | Stock is received, dispatched, transferred and returned, with on-hand quantities and low-stock alerts. No reservation against a quote is shown | The line is cut. Nothing in the film says "reserved" |

contractor, the first trade ManaGEM is built for, instead of a solar installer; and
the end line now carries the site's own headline, so the film and the landing page

## 1. Re-evaluation

### What was memorable

- **The hold as real time.** Making the viewer wait with the customer is a formal
  idea, not a joke on top of a demo.
- **The hold tune as the score.** A piece of sound the brand can own.
- **"One second."** Two words every business owner has said.

Those three survive. Almost nothing else does.

### Where it was predictable or generic

- **Chaos, then calm.** The problem-then-rewind structure is the default shape of
  every software commercial made in the last fifteen years. The audience knows the
  second half the moment the first half starts.
- **The scavenger hunt.** A spreadsheet called "FINAL_v3", a frantic walk through the
  office, a smudged whiteboard. These are internet jokes about small business, not
  observations of one.
- **A panicking owner.** It invited exactly the exaggerated acting the brief rules
  out, and it made our customer look incompetent. Owners do not identify with a man
  who cannot find a file. They identify with a man who knows his trade and is let
  down by where the information lives.
- **"CALL ENDED" after 17 seconds.** Not believable. A real client does not hang up.
  She runs out of time and goes with the quote already on her desk. The loss is
  quiet, and Round 1 made it loud.
- **"That was quick."** A button line from a hundred other ads.
- **The closing voiceover.** "Enquiries, quotes, projects, stock and finances, in one
  place" is a feature list read aloud, which the brief expressly forbids.
- **The 12-second Steadicam take.** Impressive on paper, the hardest possible shot
  for AI-assisted production, and attached to the weakest part of the idea.

### Where the tension really is

Round 1 put the tension in the search. The stronger tension is in three places it
ignored.

1. **Nobody is searching. Everybody is waiting.** The answer to one customer question
   lives with four different people. The owner waits for the office, the office waits
   for the store, the store waits for the supplier. That is the true picture of
   scattered information, and it is funnier and sadder than a man opening drawers.
2. **A deadline that is not ours.** The client has a trustees' meeting at six. Her
   clock, not our panic, drives the film.
3. **Dramatic irony.** She leaves. He does not know. He is still on the roof, still
   holding, still waiting for a number to give to someone who has gone. The audience
   knows before he does. That beat needs no acting at all.

### What makes it distinctive now

**A commercial in which, for the first twenty seconds, nobody speaks.** Four people
in four places listen to the same terrible tune. We open on the person at the end of
the chain and work backwards, so each cut reveals that the person she is waiting for
is also waiting. The viewer in an ad break hears hold music, with no voiceover and no
logo, and looks up to see what has gone wrong with their television.

### What someone remembers after one viewing

- The tune.
- The picture of a whole business on hold with itself.
- "You don't need to check?" / "I just did."

## 2. Three alternative openings

Each must work in three seconds with no score, no overacting and no effects.

### Opening A — "On hold" (recommended)

Hard cut from the previous ad into a tinny, looping hold tune, already playing. A
woman in her fifties at an office desk, desk phone on speaker, pen tapping, eyes on a
wall clock: 17:41. No words. No titles. She is waiting and now so are we.

*Why it works:* the sound is the hook. Hold music is instantly recognised, mildly
irritating and completely out of place in an ad break. It raises one question, "who
is she waiting for?", and the film's whole structure is the answer.

### Opening B — "The roof"

A very wide, still frame. A flat silver roof the size of a rugby field, Johannesburg
low on the horizon, late sun. One small figure in the middle. Wind, and nothing else.
A phone rings, tiny in all that space. He answers. We hear only his half:
"Yes. Yes, I can. …Can you hold one second?"

*Why it works:* scale and silence. It is the most cinematic of the three and
establishes the trade at once. It is slower to pose the question and depends on a
spoken line landing in a wide shot.

### Opening C — "The number"

Black. A woman's voice, close, mid-sentence, entirely real: "…so I just need your
number before six." Cut on the last word to an extreme close-up of a work-roughened
thumb pressing HOLD. The tune starts.

*Why it works:* it states the stakes fastest and is the nearest to Round 1. It is
also the most conventional, and it opens on a black frame, which costs attention on
social feeds where the first frame is the thumbnail.

**Recommendation:** Opening A for the 45 and the 30. Opening C is held in reserve for
the 15-second cutdown, where there is no time to build a mystery.

### The world

**Mokoena Waterproofing & Roofing**, Johannesburg. Fourteen staff, two bakkies, a
storeroom, an office with one administrator. Good at the work. The company's main
number rings through to the owner's mobile when the office is busy, so everyone who
waits for this business hears the same tune.

Yes. Fifteen more seconds buy three things the 30 cannot hold: the fourth link in the
chain (the storeman on hold with a supplier, which is what makes the chain absurd
instead of merely unlucky), the loop restarting, and the silence on the roof. The 45
is the master for cinema, YouTube, connected TV and LinkedIn. The 30 is cut from it
for broadcast.

### Direction

- **Camera.** Locked-off or very slow push-ins. One composed frame per location in
  the first half, like four portraits. The only handheld shot is Sizwe on the roof at
  0:27, where the frame is allowed to breathe with him. Nothing longer than six
  seconds. 2.39:1 master, with 9:16 and 1:1 protected.
- **Light.** The problem half is all one late afternoon: low gold sun on the roof,
  flat fluorescent in the office and store, cool daylight with a warm desk lamp in
  the property office. The resolution is morning, soft and clean. The grade is
  natural and slightly warm. No teal-and-orange, no "tech" blue.
- **Design.** Real wear. A supplier's wall calendar. A kettle. Drums with handwritten
  counts on the lids. Hi-vis that has been washed two hundred times. On the roof, the
  craft is visible and good: straight seams, clean laps. We are proud of this
  business.
- **Performance.** Nobody acts waiting. They wait. The comedy is in how little
  anyone does.
- **Edit.** Hard cuts only. The continuous tune is the transition.

## 4. ManaGEM's role

### How the product enters

It is not introduced. On the second morning the phone rings, Lerato's finger goes to
the HOLD key because that is what it has done a thousand times, and it stops. Her
hand goes to the keyboard instead. That half-second, a habit breaking, is the product
demonstration. Everything after it is proof.

### What the audience understands

Yesterday the answer to one question lived in four places and with four people.
Today it is on one record that the person who answers the phone can open while the
caller is still talking. The closing voiceover says it in the website's own verified
words: every job on one record.

### What is on screen, and for how long

| Shot | What we see | Source in the product material | Duration |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Search | A name typed into the "Search anything…" bar at the top of the screen | Present on every product screen; the site states it jumps to a customer, a quote number or a job | under 1 s |
| The record | An enquiry record with its quote line items and the Grand Total, a DEPOSIT PAID status and a timeline entry for the deposit | The enquiry/quote screen in the product build | about 1.5 s |
| The job | The Current Jobs list: job number, customer, foreman, start and end dates, progress | The Projects screen in the product build | about 1 s |
| Stock, optional | The product list with its STOCK column | The Products & Pricing screen | not in the recommended cuts |

The screen is always seen over Lerato's shoulder or reflected in her attention, on a
real desktop monitor, in a real office. It is never full-frame for more than a
second and a half, never animated, never floating.

### What the film deliberately does not claim

No job costing or margin per job. No labour hours or timesheets. No site capture by
foremen from a phone. No mobile app. No stock reservation. No Xero. No customer
numbers, no set-up times, no testimonials. The audit lists each of these as
unsupported or unconfirmed. Sage sync is real but is not in this film; it belongs on
the landing page.

## 5. Three endings

Each is a closing moment plus an advertising line and a call to action.

### Ending 1 — "I just did" (recommended)

**Moment.** After Lerato's answer there is a pause on the line. The caller, not quite
believing it: "You don't need to check?" Lerato, already reaching for her tea: "I
just did." Cut to the card.

**Line.** *Know where every job stands.*
**Voiceover.** "ManaGEM. Every job on one record."
**CTA.** *Start your free trial · managem.co.za*

**Why.** The exchange is the benefit, spoken by a customer. The line is the
website's own headline, so the viewer who arrives at the site reads the sentence they
just heard. It makes no claim the product cannot support.

### Ending 2 — "The rain"

**Moment.** Sizwe on the roof with his crew, phone silent in his back pocket. The
first drops of the afternoon storm land and bead on the finished membrane. He looks
up, then goes back to the seam.

**Line.** *Your business shouldn't run you.*
**Voiceover.** "ManaGEM. So you can get on with the job."
**CTA.** *Start your free trial · managem.co.za*

**Why.** The most beautiful image in the film and the truest to the original
proposition: the owner is free because the business can answer for itself. It is
softer on product. In the 45 it is used as a two-second coda before the card, under
Ending 1's line.

### Ending 3 — "The other quote"

**Moment.** Back in the property office on the second day. Anusha's desk. One quote,
ours, with a signature on it. The desk phone is in its cradle.

**Line.** *The first answer wins the job.*
**Voiceover.** "ManaGEM. Have the answer."
**CTA.** *Start your free trial · managem.co.za*

**Why.** The hardest-selling of the three, and the line is sharp. It implies the
lost client came back, which the story has not earned, and "wins the job" is a
promise about results rather than about the product.

**Recommendation.** Ending 1, with the rain from Ending 2 as the coda in the 45. The
thought behind Ending 3 is kept, in a safer form, as the voiceover at the turn: "The
job goes to whoever answers first."

## 6. Production feasibility for AI-assisted production

The restructure was made partly for this. The chain puts each character alone, in
one place, in one costume, mostly silent. That is close to the easiest thing to ask
of generated footage, where the Round 1 long take was close to the hardest.

### Assessment by area

| Area | Risk | Why, and how the treatment handles it |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Character continuity** | Low to medium | Five on-screen people, each confined to one location. Only Sizwe and Lerato recur. Lerato appears on two different days, so a change of top is correct, not an error. Fix one approved reference portrait and wardrobe sheet per character before any shot is made |
| **Facial consistency** | Medium | Sizwe is seen in four shots and Lerato in five. Keep both at medium close-up or closer whenever the face matters, avoid profile-to-frontal jumps within a scene, and generate every shot of a character in one session from the same reference. Reject any shot where age, skin texture or hairline drifts; do not try to fix drift in the grade |
| **Lip-sync** | High, but contained | The film has about nine seconds of on-camera speech. Lines are placed to avoid sync wherever possible: Anusha never speaks, Mr Jacobs never speaks, the colleague and both callers are off-camera, Lerato's "Found it" is a phone voice. Sync is needed only for Sizwe's "Mrs Pillay? …Hello?" (four words, can play partly on the back of his head or on the crew) and Lerato's answer |
| **Environmental continuity** | Medium | Four locations, each seen from one main angle. Build each from a single approved master frame, and derive the second angle from that frame, not from a fresh description. The roof needs fixed geography: skyline on one side, sun low on the other, same seam pattern. The office is seen on two days: same room, afternoon fluorescent then morning window light |
| **Camera movement** | Low | Locked-off frames and slow push-ins only, all under six seconds. No tracking through doorways, no orbit, no whip-pan. The one handheld shot is a close-up with gentle drift, which can be added in post to a stable plate |
| **Scene transitions** | Low | Hard cuts only. Continuity is carried by the tune, which is laid in the edit, not in the clips |
| **Product-screen integration** | High if generated, low if composited | Generated interfaces produce invented layouts and garbled type. Every screen in the film is a real capture from a clean ManaGEM example account, composited onto the monitor. This is also the only way to honour "do not invent product functionality" |
| **Sound design** | Low | No clip's generated audio is used. The whole soundtrack is built conventionally: one composed hold tune, re-recorded through a real phone speaker and handset for each location, recorded voices, library ambience and foley |

### The two hard shots

1. **Lerato's answer (0:35–0:41).** It carries the product, the longest sync line and
   the last word. Protect it three ways. Play the first half of the line over the
   screen inserts, so only "Thabo's team starts Monday the fourth" and "I just did"
   are on her face. Frame her three-quarter, lit by the monitor, with the receiver
   partly covering her mouth. And record the voice first with a real actor, then
   drive the sync from that recording.
   *Recommendation to the board:* consider shooting this one set-up conventionally.
   One actor, one desk, one monitor, half a day. It is the brand moment of the film,
   and a real performance and a real screen here would lift the credibility of every
   generated shot around it. The same actor's likeness then becomes the reference for
   her other shots.
2. **Hands and phones.** The finger stopping above the HOLD key, Anusha's key press,
   Sizwe switching calls. Hands on small devices are where generated footage most
   often fails. Keep these as short inserts, hold them still, composite the phone
   display, and be prepared to shoot them as practical inserts on a tabletop.

### How each shot is made

**G** = generated as an independent shot. **C** = conventional compositing over a
generated or filmed plate. **E** = created in the edit or the sound mix.
**P** = strong candidate for a practical insert.

| # | Shot | Method | Note |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | Anusha at her desk, waiting | G | Silent portrait. Wall clock face is C, so the time is exact and legible |
| 2 | Sizwe on the roof, waiting, crew behind | G | Establishes roof geography; becomes the master frame for shots 6, 9 and 14 |
| 3 | Lerato, receiver at shoulder | G | Monitor content is C: three ordinary quote files, not ManaGEM |
| 4 | Mr Jacobs counting tins | G | Handwritten numbers on lids are C if they need to be legible; otherwise leave soft |

| 6 | Anusha ends the call and leaves with the other quote | G + C | The key press is a separate insert (G or P). The quote's letterhead is C and must not resemble a real company |
| 7 | Clock at 17:58 | C | Same clock plate as shot 1, new time |
| 8 | Sizwe, close, switches calls: "Mrs Pillay? …Hello?" | G | Sync from recorded voice. Handheld drift added in post |
| 9 | Crew behind him, stopped, watching | G | Cutaway that also protects shot 8's sync |
| 10 | Office, morning, phone rings, Lerato lifts receiver | G or P | Same room as shot 3, morning light |

### Sound, specifically

- **The tune.** One original eight-bar composition, deliberately a little cheap,
  with a clean loop point. Cleared for broadcast and owned by the brand. Not a
  library hold track and not a soundalike of a known one.
- **Worldising.** The same recording is played back through a desk speakerphone, a
  mobile handset and a second handset, and re-recorded, so that each location has
  its own true version of it. The cut between locations is heard as much as seen.
- **The stop.** When Anusha presses the key the tune cuts mid-phrase, on an
  unresolved note. The band version on the end card resolves it.
- **Voices.** Real South African voice actors for every line, recorded before any
  sync shot is generated. Phone voices are processed from clean recordings.
- **Ambience.** Wind and a gas torch on the roof. Air-conditioning in the property
  office. A fridge and a distant radio in the store. Rain. All from recorded
  libraries or foley, laid by hand.

### Honest limits

- The current capability of specific generation tools was not tested for this
  treatment. Risk ratings are judgements about the type of shot, to be confirmed by a
  short technical test of shots 2, 8 and 14 before the full production is committed.
- If the test of shot 14 does not hold up at broadcast scale, the fallback is the
  practical shoot described above, not a weaker script.
